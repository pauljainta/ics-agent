General water-distribution domain primer for a target-blind attacker.

- Water-distribution systems usually couple a supply stage, intermediate storage, consumer-facing storage, and a return or recirculation path.
- Elevated and downstream tanks buffer demand shocks, so the effect of a control change may arrive with lag and then propagate across multiple storage zones.
- Pumps add transfer energy while valves gate delivery paths, which means supply and routing changes often matter more over trends than in a single sample.
- Multiple feed or return paths can mask early impact because one path may temporarily compensate for another until inventory buffers are exhausted.
- Quality dosing near the supply side can influence later storage behavior indirectly and over a longer horizon than a simple on or off command.
- Level, flow, pressure, and alarm signals should be read together because sustained imbalance between replenishment and demand is often more dangerous than a momentary excursion.
- High storage, low storage, starvation, oscillation, and unstable routing are physically harmful patterns even when the exact unsafe boundary is unknown.
- Return or recirculation behavior can either stabilize inventory or spread the consequence of a bad control decision to another part of the system.
